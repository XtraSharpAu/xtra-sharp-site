### Scissors Sharpening — additional section: "Bevel vs. Convex — What's the Difference?"

> Bevel-edge scissors have a single flat angle ground into the blade — the standard edge type for most household and everyday grooming scissors. Convex-edge scissors have a rounded, hollow-ground edge that produces an exceptionally sharp, smooth cut — common on premium hairdressing shears. Convex sharpening takes more time and precision, which is reflected in the price difference shown on the [Pricing page](/pricing).

### Garden Tools — additional section: "Why Sharp Garden Tools Matter"

> A sharp blade makes a clean cut, which heals faster and is less likely to introduce disease into a plant than a ragged cut from a dull blade. This applies to secateurs and hedge shears just as much as it does to axes — the same water-cooled sharpening process used on knives is used here to protect the tool's edge from heat damage.

### Processor Blades — additional section: "For Butchers, Restaurants & Food Businesses"

> Processor blades that lose their edge don't just cut poorly — they can put extra strain on the motor and produce uneven, torn product instead of a clean cut. Regular sharpening keeps commercial processor equipment running efficiently, whether it's a single home unit or a large industrial blade used daily in a commercial kitchen or butchery.
