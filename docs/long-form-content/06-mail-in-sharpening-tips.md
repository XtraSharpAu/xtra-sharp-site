# Mail-In Sharpening — Tips Beyond Packing

*(For the step-by-step packing instructions themselves, see the existing guide in `docs/marketing-pack/blog-posts/06-how-to-pack-knives-mail-in.md` and the live `/mail-in` page. This article covers the decisions around mail-in that packing instructions don't.)*

## Short (~160 words)

Mail-in sharpening works best when you plan around it rather than just packing and posting. Batch multiple items into one parcel rather than sending things separately — it's more efficient for postage and for turnaround. Choose your satchel size based on both the number of items and their total bulk, not just what looks like it'll physically fit (a tightly-stuffed satchel is more likely to be damaged in transit). Always contact first to confirm pricing and satchel size rather than guessing — this avoids delays if the wrong size arrives. Factor in transit time each way, not just the 1–2 business day sharpening turnaround, when working out your total timeline.

## Medium (~380 words)

**Batch your items.** If you've got several things that need sharpening — a few kitchen knives, a pair of secateurs, a pair of scissors — send them together in one parcel rather than posting separately. It's more efficient on postage, and everything comes back to you at once.

**Choose satchel size deliberately.** It's tempting to just pick "whatever fits," but a satchel stuffed to its absolute limit is more likely to be damaged or have contents shift during transit. If you're unsure, contact first — pricing and satchel size are confirmed before you post, not guessed at.

**Plan around total transit time, not just sharpening turnaround.** The 1–2 business day figure refers to sharpening time after your parcel arrives — it doesn't include the days your parcel spends in transit each way. If you need something back by a specific date, factor in postage time in both directions, not just the sharpening window.

**Include a prepaid return satchel, or don't — but decide in advance.** You can either include a prepaid Australia Post satchel for the return trip, or have postage added to your final total. Either works, but deciding which one you want before posting avoids a delay while it gets sorted out.

**Note anything unusual about condition.** If an item has a known chip, rust spot, or other damage, mention it in your note or when you make contact — this helps set the right expectation and avoids any surprises when a quote is confirmed.

**Double-check your return details are legible.** Your return address, phone number, and any notes should be clearly written and included inside the parcel, not just relying on the external shipping label, in case that label is ever damaged in transit.

## Long (~520 words)

Packing instructions tell you *how* to protect your items in transit — this is about the decisions around mail-in sharpening that make the whole process smoother, which packing guides don't usually cover.

### Batch, Don't Trickle

If you've got multiple items that need attention — say, a couple of kitchen knives and a pair of secateurs — it's almost always better to send them together in one parcel rather than posting them as you notice each one going blunt. Consolidating into one mail-in reduces total postage cost, means one turnaround window instead of several, and means everything comes back to you at once rather than in scattered parcels.

### Choosing the Right Satchel Size

It's tempting to pick whatever satchel looks like it'll physically fit your items, but that's not quite the right test. A satchel packed to its absolute limit is more prone to damage in transit — seams stress, and there's less cushioning room around your items. The better approach is to contact first and confirm the right size based on both the number of items and their combined bulk once properly wrapped (which adds real volume beyond the bare items themselves).

### Understanding the Real Timeline

The commonly quoted "1–2 business days" refers specifically to sharpening turnaround *after your parcel arrives* — it's not the total time from when you post to when you receive items back. A realistic total timeline is: transit time to reach the sharpening location, plus 1–2 business days for the sharpening itself, plus transit time back to you. If you need items back by a specific date for an event or a busy period, work backward from that date accounting for both transit legs, not just the sharpening window.

### Return Postage: Decide Up Front

You have two options for the return trip: include a prepaid Australia Post satchel with your parcel, or have the postage cost added to your final total. Both are straightforward, but deciding which you want before you post — rather than during a back-and-forth after your parcel has already arrived — keeps things moving without an extra delay.

### Flag Anything Unusual

If an item has known damage, unusual wear, or you're not sure whether it's even suitable for sharpening, mention it — either in a note included with the parcel or by contacting before you post. This means less back-and-forth once your items are being assessed, and no surprise on pricing if condition affects the quote.

### Protect Your Contact Details, Not Just the Shipping Label

External shipping labels occasionally get damaged or become illegible in transit. Including your return address, phone number, and any relevant notes on a slip of paper *inside* the parcel means your details survive even if the outside of the box takes some damage.

### The Bottom Line

Good packing keeps your items physically safe in transit — good planning (batching, satchel sizing, realistic timelines, decided-in-advance return postage) keeps the whole mail-in process fast and free of unnecessary back-and-forth. Both matter for a smooth experience.
