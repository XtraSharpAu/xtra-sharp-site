## Medium (~380 words)

Clipper blades are actually two blades working as a matched pair — the cutting blade (which moves) and the comb blade (which stays still) — and most of what determines whether a clipper "cuts well" comes down to how precisely those two blades interact, not just how sharp either one is individually.

**Daily and weekly care:**
- Clean hair, oil, and skin debris off after every single use — buildup accelerates wear and can affect how the blades sit against each other.
- Oil regularly, following your clipper manufacturer's specific guidance — a dry blade wears faster and runs hotter.
- Check visual alignment before use — if the teeth on the two blades don't line up cleanly, something has shifted and needs attention.
- Store blades separately from other metal objects to avoid nicks from incidental contact.

**Signs a blade needs professional attention**, beyond what daily care can fix:
- Pulling or tugging instead of a clean glide
- Noticeably more heat during use
- Visible nicks or unevenness along the edge
- Needing to go over the same area repeatedly for a clean result

## Long (~600 words)

Ask any groomer what makes the biggest difference to how smoothly a job goes, and blade condition is near the top of the list — not just sharpness, but the whole condition of the blade pair.

### The Blade Pair, Not Just "The Blade"

A clipper blade set consists of a cutting blade (the moving part, usually the top blade) and a comb blade (the stationary part beneath it). Hair is cut in the gap between the teeth of the two blades as the cutting blade oscillates. This means the performance of a clipper blade depends on:

- How sharp each individual blade's edge is
- How precisely the two blades align and track against each other
- The tension holding them together, which affects how cleanly the shearing action happens

A blade that's individually sharp but misaligned with its pair will still pull and drag — which is exactly why professional clipper blade sharpening addresses alignment and tracking together with the edge, not the edge alone.

### What You Can Do Between Sharpens

**Clean after every single use.** Hair, skin oils, and product residue build up fast, and left in place, they accelerate wear on both the edge and the moving mechanism.

**Oil regularly.** Follow your specific clipper manufacturer's oiling guidance — a dry blade wears faster and generates more heat during use, which is uncomfortable for the animal and hard on the blade itself.

**Check alignment visually before each use.** If the teeth on the top and bottom blades look offset rather than cleanly matched, that's a sign something has shifted — continuing to use a misaligned blade both performs poorly and can accelerate damage.

**Store separately.** Loose blades knocking against other metal objects in a bag or drawer is a common, avoidable cause of small nicks that shouldn't be there.

### Recognising When It's Time for a Professional Sharpen

- Pulling, tugging, or dragging instead of a clean glide through the coat
- The blade running noticeably hotter than a fresh one during normal use
- Visible nicks, chips, or unevenness along the cutting edge
- Needing multiple passes over the same area to get a clean result that used to take one pass
