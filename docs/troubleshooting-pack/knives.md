# Knife Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Edge dulls again within days of sharpening | Burr not fully removed during a previous (non-professional) sharpen, or a very hard cutting surface (glass/stone board) | Switch to a wood/plastic board; if it recurs after our sharpening specifically, let us know — that shouldn't happen |
| Knife slips/rocks instead of cutting straight | Bevel ground unevenly, or handle/tang issue unrelated to the edge | If it's edge-related, a professional sharpen restores an even bevel; if it persists after sharpening, the issue may be the blade's straightness itself |
| Visible white line along the edge under light | Edge is dull — light reflects off a blunt edge, not a sharp one | Time for a sharpen |
| Knife feels "grabby" or catches on soft food | Edge may have micro-chips or an uneven burr | A light hone may help temporarily; a full sharpen addresses the underlying issue |
| Rust spots appearing on the blade | Moisture left on the blade, especially on carbon steel, or inadequate drying after washing | Dry thoroughly after every wash; light oiling helps on carbon steel |
| Blade discoloured (straw/blue tint) near the edge | Heat damage from an aggressive dry-grinding sharpen (not from water-cooled sharpening) | This indicates temper damage — the edge may not hold as well going forward; water-cooled re-sharpening won't undo existing heat damage but prevents further damage |
| Tip broken or chipped | Impact damage — dropped, used to pry something, or hit bone/hard material | Can often be reshaped during sharpening, though this changes the blade's profile slightly — ask for an assessment |
| Handle loose or wobbling | Wear at the tang/handle join, unrelated to the blade edge | This is a knife repair issue, not a sharpening one — a bladesmith or knife repair specialist is the right resource |
| Knife pulls to one side when cutting | Uneven bevel from inconsistent home sharpening over time | A professional sharpen restores an even, correctly-centred bevel |
| Smells or discolouration transferring between foods | Residue not fully washed off, or a blade that needs more thorough cleaning between uses (not a sharpening issue) | Wash and dry more thoroughly after each use |
| Serrated section not cutting cleanly | Serrations dull unevenly and need a different sharpening technique than a straight edge | Mention it's serrated when booking — this is handled carefully to preserve the tooth pattern |
| Knife feels "thin"/fragile after many sharpens over the years | Repeated sharpening gradually removes material — expected over a long service life, not a fault | Normal wear from years of use; eventually a blade may need replacing rather than further sharpening |
