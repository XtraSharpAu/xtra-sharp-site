# "How to Prepare Your Items" Guides (10)

Practical, item-specific prep guidance for drop-off or mail-in. General packing steps are already established on `/mail-in`; these guides add item-specific detail layered on top of that base process.

## 1. Kitchen & Butcher Knives

- Wash and dry the blade before dropping off/posting (removes food residue that can interfere with sharpening).
- Wrap the blade in a tea towel or similar, tip protected, before placing in a bag or box.
- If mailing, fold cardboard over the tip as extra protection against puncturing the packaging.

## 2. Hunting Knives & Axes

- Note any known chips, rust, or damage when you contact us — condition affects pricing on these items.
- Sheathed knives can be sent sheathed; unsheathed blades need extra wrapping (cardboard + cloth) around the edge and tip.
- For axes, wrap the head separately from the handle if loose-fitting to avoid movement in transit.

## 3. Hairdressing & Grooming Scissors

- Wipe down before drop-off/mail-in — hair and product residue can be sharpened around but a clean pair is easier to inspect.
- Keep each pair in its own protective case or wrapped individually if sending multiple pairs — don't let blades rub against each other in transit.
- Mention if you know whether your scissors are bevel or convex edge (we can confirm if you're not sure).

## 4. Sewing & Household Scissors

- No special prep needed beyond standard wrapping — these are generally straightforward jobs.
- If multiple pairs, label them if you want them kept separate (e.g. "fabric only" vs "general use") so tension/edge type stays matched to intended use.

## 5. Clipper Blades

- Remove the blade from the clipper body before sending, unless instructed otherwise.
- Wipe off excess hair and oil before packing.
- Wrap each blade individually — blades knocking together in transit can chip the edge.

## 6. Garden Tools (Secateurs, Hedge Shears)

- Clean off dirt, sap, and plant residue before drop-off/mail-in.
- Keep any pivot bolts/screws finger-tight so the tool doesn't fall apart in the box.
- Wrap cutting edges separately from handles if the tool disassembles.

## 7. Axes & Tomahawks (Garden/Outdoor Use)

- Same as hunting knives — protect the edge with cardboard/cloth and note any visible damage.
- If the handle is loose or cracked, mention it — this may affect what work is safe to do.

## 8. Processor Blades (Home)

- Wash and fully dry before packing — food residue left on a blade in transit isn't pleasant to receive.
- Wrap each blade individually if sending more than one, with tips/points protected.

## 9. Processor Blades (Commercial/Industrial)

- Contact first for these — size and packing requirements vary too much for one-size-fits-all instructions, and freight (rather than a standard satchel) may be needed for larger blades.
- Provide the make/model or dimensions if known, to help confirm pricing and the right transport method.

## 10. General Multi-Item / Batch Drop-Off

- List everything you're bringing/sending in advance if it's more than a few items — helps us prepare and quote accurately.
- Group by type (all knives together, all scissors together) to speed up both inspection and hand-back.
- For businesses sending a regular batch, keep a consistent labelling system (initials, department, etc.) so items don't get mixed up between visits.
