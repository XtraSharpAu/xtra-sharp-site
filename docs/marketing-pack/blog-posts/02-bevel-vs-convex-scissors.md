# Bevel vs. Convex Scissors Sharpening — What's the Difference?

If you've ever had scissors sharpened and been quoted two different prices depending on the edge type, you've run into the bevel-versus-convex distinction. Here's what it actually means, and why it's worth knowing before you book.

## Bevel Edges — The Standard

A bevel edge is ground with a single flat angle along the blade. It's the standard edge type on most household scissors, general-purpose sewing scissors, and a lot of everyday grooming scissors. It's reliable, relatively quick to sharpen well, and holds up fine for general cutting tasks.

## Convex Edges — The Premium Option

A convex edge is hollow-ground into a smooth, rounded curve rather than a flat angle. Done properly, it produces an exceptionally sharp, smooth cutting action — which is why it's the standard on premium hairdressing shears, where the quality of the cut directly affects the finished haircut. The trade-off is that convex sharpening takes considerably more time, precision, and a different technique than bevel sharpening — which is reflected in the price.

## Why the Price Difference Exists

At Xtra Sharp, hairdressing scissors sharpened with a bevel edge start from $50, while convex-edge hairdressing scissors are $70. The gap isn't arbitrary — convex sharpening genuinely takes longer to get right, because there's no flat reference angle to grind against; the entire curve has to be maintained evenly along the whole blade length, by hand-guided machine work, to avoid flattening or unevenness that would ruin the scissors' performance.

Grooming scissors follow the same pattern at a smaller scale: bevel from $25, convex from $35.

## How to Tell Which You Have

If you're not sure whether your scissors are bevel or convex, don't worry — it's something we can confirm for you. As a rough guide, though: if your scissors came from a hairdressing supply company as a "professional" pair, there's a good chance they're convex. Everyday household, sewing, or craft scissors are almost always bevel.

## Machine-Guided Precision, Either Way

Whichever edge type your scissors have, the sharpening process at Xtra Sharp is machine-guided — meaning the angle, ride line (how the two blades meet and glide against each other), and tension are all restored correctly, not just the edge itself. A scissors sharpen that only addresses the edge but ignores tension or ride line can leave you with a pair that looks sharp but doesn't actually cut cleanly — it's the whole system that needs to be right.

## Bottom Line

If your scissors are dragging, catching, or requiring more hand pressure than they used to, it's time for a sharpen regardless of edge type. Knowing whether you're working with bevel or convex just helps set the right expectation on price and turnaround before you book.
