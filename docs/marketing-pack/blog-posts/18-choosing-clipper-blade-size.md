# Choosing the Right Clipper Blade Size for Your Grooming Business

Clipper blade sharpening only helps if the blade being sharpened is the right one for the job. A quick guide to blade sizing for groomers building out or refreshing their kit.

## A5 Blades — The Standard Workhorse

A5-size blades are the most common blade format in professional grooming and fit the widest range of clipper bodies. Most general-purpose coat work — the bulk of a typical grooming day — runs through A5 blades, which is why they're usually the first blades a groomer stocks multiples of.

## Wide Blades — Faster Coverage on Bigger Coats

Wide blades cover more surface area per pass than standard A5 blades, which matters for larger dogs or breeds with a lot of coat to get through. The trade-off is manoeuvrability — a wide blade is faster in open areas but less precise around tight spots (paws, faces, ears) than a standard-width blade.

## Large-Animal Blades — Built for Different Coat and Skin

Large-animal blades (for livestock and larger animals outside typical companion-animal grooming) are a different category again — generally built for coarser coat and different skin conditions than a dog or cat coat. If your work spans companion animals and larger animals, these need to be sharpened and treated as a separate blade category, not interchangeably with A5s.

## Why Blade Count Matters as Much as Blade Type

A single blade of each size gets you through a day, but any groomer running a full book knows blades need rotation — a blade in use is a blade that's accumulating wear, heat, and hair buildup. Having a second (or third) blade of your most-used size in the cycle means one can be in for sharpening while another is in the chair, rather than a gap in your kit while you wait.

## Matching Blade Condition to Coat Type

A blade that's slightly past its best still works fine on a light, easy coat — but on a thick, matted, or double coat, a marginal blade shows its limits fast (pulling, snagging, uneven length). If you're noticing blades needing sharpening more often on certain jobs specifically, that's often the coat type revealing a blade edge that was already borderline, not a fault with the animal or the groomer.

## The Bottom Line

Getting blade size and blade count right for your specific client mix does as much for your day-to-day work as sharpening quality does — the two work together.
