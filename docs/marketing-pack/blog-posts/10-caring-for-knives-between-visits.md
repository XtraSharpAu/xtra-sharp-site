# Caring for Your Knives Between Professional Sharpening Visits

Professional sharpening restores an edge properly, but what you do with your knives in between visits has a real effect on how long that edge lasts — and how much work is needed next time.

## Wash by Hand, Not in the Dishwasher

Dishwashers are hard on knife edges: the combination of heat, harsh detergent, and knives knocking against other cutlery and racks during the wash cycle dulls and can even chip a blade faster than regular use alone. Hand-washing, drying immediately, and putting the knife away straight after is a small habit that makes a real difference over months and years.

## Store Them Properly

Loose knives rattling around in a drawer are one of the fastest ways to dull an edge — every time the blade contacts another hard object, it takes a tiny amount of damage. A knife block, a magnetic strip, or individual blade guards all protect the edge far better than a communal drawer.

## Use the Right Cutting Surface

The cutting board matters more than most people realise:

- **Wooden or plastic boards**: safe for your knife's edge — they give slightly under the blade.
- **Glass, stone, or ceramic boards**: look nice but are genuinely harsh on an edge — every cut is effectively grinding the blade against a hard, unforgiving surface.
- **Metal or unfinished stone counters directly**: worst option of all — avoid cutting directly on any hard surface.

## Hone Between Sharpens

A honing steel doesn't sharpen a knife — it doesn't remove metal — but it does realign the microscopic edge that bends slightly out of true with regular use. A few strokes on a honing steel every few uses keeps that edge performing closer to its just-sharpened state for longer, delaying (though not replacing) the need for a full professional sharpen.

## Don't Use Your Knife as a Multi-Tool

Prying open packaging, cutting through bone or frozen food without the right knife for the job, or using a kitchen knife on cardboard all put stress on an edge that's designed for clean slicing through food, not general-purpose cutting. Keep a separate utility knife or scissors on hand for anything that isn't actual food prep.

## Know When You've Crossed From "Care" to "Time for a Sharpen"

None of the above replaces a professional sharpen — it just extends the life of one. Once you notice a knife sliding off a tomato's skin instead of biting in, or needing more pressure than it used to for everyday cuts, that's the point where honing and good care alone won't fix it, and it's time to book a proper sharpen.
