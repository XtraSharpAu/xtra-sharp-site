# Why a Sharp Pruning Tool Is Better for Your Garden

It's easy to think of blunt secateurs as just an inconvenience — a bit more effort to get through a branch. But the condition of your cutting tools actually affects how healthy your plants stay after every cut.

## Clean Cuts Heal Faster

When secateurs, hedge shears, or a pruning saw are sharp, they make a single, clean cut through plant tissue. A blunt tool, by contrast, tends to crush and tear rather than slice — the cell walls around the cut are damaged over a wider area than the cut itself. Plants heal clean cuts faster and with less stress than ragged, crushed ones, which matters for everything from a rose bush to a fruit tree.

## Ragged Cuts Invite Disease

A torn, crushed cut leaves more damaged tissue exposed, which creates a larger, more vulnerable entry point for fungal and bacterial infections. This is especially relevant for anyone pruning fruit trees or roses, where disease susceptibility after pruning is a genuine, well-known concern among gardeners. A clean cut from a sharp blade simply gives pathogens less to work with.

## It's Easier on You, Too

A sharp blade does the cutting — a blunt one makes your hand and wrist do the work instead, through extra squeezing force. Over a long pruning session, that difference adds up, and it's a real factor in hand fatigue and even repetitive strain for anyone doing a lot of garden work.

## When to Get Ahead of It

The best time to sharpen garden tools is before the growing season ramps up, not mid-way through when you're relying on them daily. A pre-spring sharpen means your tools are ready exactly when you need them most, rather than discovering they're blunt halfway through a big pruning job.
