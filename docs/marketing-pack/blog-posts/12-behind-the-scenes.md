# What Actually Happens to Your Item at Xtra Sharp

When you drop off a knife or post in a pair of scissors, it's easy to picture "sharpening" as one simple action. In reality, your item goes through a genuine multi-step process before it comes back to you — here's what that actually looks like.

## Arrival and Assessment

Whether dropped off or arriving by post, every item is checked against what was booked and assessed for condition before anything else happens. This is where existing damage, an unusual edge angle, or anything that needs a conversation before proceeding gets caught — not after the fact.

## Choosing the Right Approach

Different items get genuinely different treatment. Knives, garden tools, and processor blades go through water-cooled sharpening, which keeps the blade's temperature managed throughout grinding to protect the steel's temper. Scissors and clipper blades go through a machine-guided process addressing angle, alignment, and tension together, not just the edge.

## The Actual Grinding

This is the part people picture when they think "sharpening" — but it's really several steps: correcting any existing damage or angle drift, forming the actual cutting edge, and removing the microscopic burr that every sharpening pass raises (a step that's easy to rush, and exactly why some lower-quality sharpening leaves you with an edge that goes dull again within days).

## Cleaning and Quality Check

Before anything gets packaged up or handed back, it's cleaned of sharpening residue and checked against a real quality standard — edge evenness, no remaining burr, no heat damage, correct angle, and for scissors and clipper blades, tension and alignment confirmed as well as the edge itself.

## Getting It Back to You

For drop-off, that's a call or text once it's ready, usually same day or next day. For mail-in, it's careful repackaging — edge protected, wrapped individually if there are multiple items — and posting back with tracked Australia Post return postage, with the tracking number sent straight to you.

## Why This Matters

None of this is unusual or exclusive — it's simply what proper sharpening actually involves when none of the steps get skipped. Understanding the real process is part of understanding why a genuinely good sharpen holds up for months, not days, and why the price reflects real time and attention rather than a quick pass over a wheel.

## The Bottom Line

Sharpening looks simple from the outside, but a proper job is a genuine sequence of assessment, the right method for the item, careful grinding and burr removal, and a real quality check before anything comes back to you — which is exactly what happens to every item that comes through Xtra Sharp.
