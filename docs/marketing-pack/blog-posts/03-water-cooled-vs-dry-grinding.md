# Water-Cooled vs. Dry Grinding: Why It Matters for Your Knives

Not every sharpening service uses the same method, and the difference isn't cosmetic — it affects how well your knife performs and how long that edge actually lasts.

## What Happens When Steel Gets Too Hot

Every knife blade is tempered during manufacturing — a heat-treatment process that gives the steel the right balance of hardness (so it holds an edge) and toughness (so it doesn't chip or snap). That temper is achieved at a specific, controlled temperature. If a blade is subsequently heated too much during sharpening — which is exactly what happens with fast, high-friction dry grinding — the steel near the edge can lose some of that carefully-balanced temper.

The result is a blade that might look freshly sharpened, but:

- Doesn't hold its edge as long under normal use
- Is more prone to small chips along the edge
- In more extreme cases, can show visible discolouration (a "bluing" or straw colour along the very edge) — a clear sign of heat damage

## How Water-Cooled Sharpening Avoids This

Water-cooled sharpening keeps a constant flow of water across the blade and the grinding surface throughout the process. This carries heat away as fast as it's generated, keeping the steel at a safe temperature from start to finish — so the factory temper stays intact, and the edge you get back is both sharp and durable, not just sharp on the day.

## Why This Matters More for Some Items Than Others

This isn't just a knife issue. At Xtra Sharp, water-cooled sharpening is used across every category where it matters: kitchen and butcher knives, hunting knives, large knives, garden tools like secateurs and hedge shears, axes and tomahawks, and processor blades. Anywhere the steel's edge-holding ability matters for real-world performance — not just for how the blade looks straight after sharpening — water-cooled sharpening is the right process.

Scissors and clipper blades are a different case: they're sharpened using a machine-guided process rather than water-cooled grinding, because the priority there is precision (correct angle, ride line, alignment and tension) rather than removing heat from a thick, single-bevel blade — a different problem with a different solution.

## What to Ask If You're Not Sure

If you're getting knives or garden tools sharpened somewhere and you're not sure what method is used, it's a completely reasonable question to ask. A sharpening service that can't tell you how they manage heat during grinding may be using a fast dry-grinding process that trades long-term edge quality for speed.

## The Bottom Line

A knife is only as good as the steel behind its edge. Water-cooled sharpening protects that steel instead of quietly damaging it in the process of "fixing" it — which is exactly why it's the standard process used on every knife, garden tool, and processor blade sharpened at Xtra Sharp.
