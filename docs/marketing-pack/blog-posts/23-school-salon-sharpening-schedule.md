# Setting Up a School or Salon Sharpening Schedule

Schools and salons both go through a lot of scheduled, repeated cutting activity — and both benefit from moving sharpening onto a recurring cycle rather than handling it item by item.

## Schools: Predictable Terms, Predictable Wear

School scissors and craft or workshop tools see concentrated use across a term and then sit largely idle over school holidays. That pattern makes scheduling straightforward — a sharpening cycle timed to land at the start or end of a term, rather than mid-term when tools are in daily use, keeps everything ready to go without disrupting a class relying on a full set of usable scissors.

## Salons: Volume Varies by Chair, Not Just by Week

A hairdressing or grooming salon's sharpening needs scale with chairs and bookings, not just time — a busy multi-stylist salon works through edge life faster than a solo operator, even over the same calendar period. A recurring schedule sized to actual chair volume (not a generic "every few months" assumption) keeps every stylist working with consistent tools rather than some sharper than others.

## Why Item-By-Item Booking Breaks Down at This Scale

Sending in one pair of scissors or one set of clippers whenever it's noticed as dull works fine for an individual. For a school with dozens of scissors, or a salon with multiple stylists each running their own kit, item-by-item booking means constant small handovers instead of one predictable, planned cycle — more admin, more chance something gets missed until it's a problem.

## What a Recurring Cycle Looks Like in Practice

A batch pick-up or drop-off, on an agreed interval, covering everything due for that round in one visit — rather than a trickle of individual bookings throughout the term or month. Batch pricing applies to these bulk cycles, which typically works out more cost-effective than the same volume of items sharpened individually over time.

Whether it's a school managing a shared set of classroom scissors or a salon keeping every stylist's kit consistently sharp, moving from reactive, individual bookings to a scheduled batch cycle is usually simpler to manage and better value than handling it item by item.
