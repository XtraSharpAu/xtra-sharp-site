# How to Pack Your Knives for Mail-In Sharpening (Step-by-Step)

Mail-in sharpening means your knives spend time in transit before they ever reach a sharpening bench — so getting the packing right matters, both for safety (nobody wants a blade poking through a satchel) and to make sure your knives arrive in the same condition they left in.

## Step 1: Contact First

Before you post anything, get in touch to confirm pricing, turnaround time, and the right satchel size for what you're sending. This avoids any surprises and means everything's ready to go the moment your parcel arrives.

## Step 2: Wash and Dry Each Knife

Food residue left on a blade in transit isn't pleasant for anyone handling the parcel, and it can also interfere with the sharpening process itself. A quick wash and thorough dry before packing takes a minute and makes a real difference.

## Step 3: Wrap Each Item Separately

Don't bundle multiple knives together in one wrap — blades knocking against each other in transit can chip or dull edges, and loose blades are a real safety risk to whoever opens the parcel. Use soft materials: tea towels, rags, newspaper, or bubble wrap all work well.

## Step 4: Protect Every Blade Tip

This is the step people most often skip, and it's the most important one for safety. A blade tip pointed toward the edge of a wrap or box can punch straight through packaging in transit. Fold a piece of cardboard over the tip, or add an extra layer or two of cloth or bubble wrap specifically at the point, before wrapping the rest of the blade.

## Step 5: Pack Into a Strong Box

Loose items shifting around inside a box during transit are far more likely to damage packaging (and themselves) than items packed tightly. Use a sturdy box — not a flimsy satchel-only setup for anything with weight or sharp points — and pack your wrapped knives in so they can't move around.

## Step 6: Include Your Details

Inside the box, include your return address, phone number, email address, and any notes about your items (e.g. "please go easy on this one, it's got a chip near the tip"). This protects you if the outer label is ever damaged, and it means we have everything needed to contact you without delay.

## Step 7: Include Your Return Satchel

Pop a prepaid Australia Post satchel in with your parcel for the return trip — this is the easiest way to get your knives back with tracking. If you'd rather not organise a satchel yourself, just let us know and postage can be added to your final total instead.

## Step 8: Send It

Once everything's packed, send it to the address provided after booking. You'll hear from us once it arrives, and again once it's on its way back — usually within 1–2 business days of arrival for the sharpening itself.

## The Bottom Line

None of this takes long, but skipping a step — especially tip protection — is how knives get damaged or, worse, injure someone in transit. A few extra minutes of careful packing means your knives arrive safely, get sharpened properly, and come back to you exactly how you'd want them.
