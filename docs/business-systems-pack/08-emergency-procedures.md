# Emergency Procedures

Operational response procedures — what to actually *do*. For the customer-facing conversation in the overlapping scenarios (damaged/incorrect items), see `01-customer-service-scripts.md` Sections 8–9; this section covers the business-continuity and decision-making side.

## 1. Machine Failure

1. Stop work immediately if equipment fails mid-job — don't attempt to push through a job on malfunctioning equipment.
2. Assess whether it's a quick fix (per the equipment's own manufacturer troubleshooting) or requires servicing/repair.
3. If any bookings are affected, contact those customers proactively per `01-customer-service-scripts.md` Section 7 (Handling Delays) — don't wait for them to notice.
4. Keep a record of the failure and resolution — recurring failures on the same equipment are a signal it's due for replacement or a more thorough service, feeding into `03-checklists.md`'s Machine Maintenance checklist.

## 2. Power Outage

1. Stop any in-progress work safely — don't leave equipment mid-operation.
2. If items are mid-process, assess once power returns whether they're safe to continue or need reassessment.
3. If the outage affects turnaround for booked jobs, notify affected customers proactively (same principle as Machine Failure above).
4. For planned outages (known maintenance in the area), reschedule appointments ahead of time rather than having customers arrive to a closed workspace.

## 3. Damaged Item Received

*(Operational response — for the customer conversation itself, see `01-customer-service-scripts.md` Section 8.)*

1. Do not proceed with any work until the damage is documented (photographed) and the customer has confirmed how to proceed.
2. Determine whether the item is safe to sharpen at all, per `/terms`' "if an item is unsafe or structurally compromised, sharpening may be refused."
3. Record whether the damage was present at intake (customer's responsibility/pre-existing) vs. a genuine concern about handling during transit or by Xtra Sharp — these have different resolutions per `04-business-policies.md`'s Damage Policy.

## 4. Incorrect Item Received

*(Operational response — for the customer conversation itself, see `01-customer-service-scripts.md` Section 9.)*

1. Do not proceed with any work on an item that doesn't match the booking until confirmed with the customer.
2. Hold the item securely and separately until clarified — don't return it to a general queue where it could be mixed up further.
3. Update the booking record once clarified, so pricing and expectations match what's actually being done.

## 5. Lost Mail-In Parcel

1. **Outbound (customer → Xtra Sharp)**: if a customer reports posting an item that hasn't arrived within a reasonable window, advise them to lodge an enquiry with Australia Post directly (per `/terms`, outbound transit is the customer's responsibility, and Australia Post's own tracking/claims process — including any compensation — is governed by Australia Post's service terms, not something to promise on Xtra Sharp's behalf without checking Australia Post's actual current policy at the time).
2. **Return (Xtra Sharp → customer)**: since return postage is always tracked, check the tracking status first. If it shows genuinely lost (not just delayed) rather than in transit, Xtra Sharp should lodge the enquiry/claim with Australia Post directly, since Xtra Sharp is the sender for that leg — keep the customer informed throughout rather than leaving them to chase.
3. In either case, communicate proactively and honestly with the customer about status — don't go silent while a parcel issue is being investigated.
4. Once resolved (found, or confirmed lost), document the outcome, including whether/how any resolution (re-sharpening the customer's other items at no charge as a goodwill gesture, for example) is offered — this is a genuine business decision, not something to pre-commit to in this document.

## 6. Customer Dispute

1. Listen fully before responding — see `01-customer-service-scripts.md` Section 6 for the conversational approach.
2. Document the dispute (what's being disputed, any relevant photos/records from intake or QC) — this protects both parties by creating an accurate record rather than relying on memory later.
3. Refer to the relevant policy in `04-business-policies.md` (Warranty, Refund, or Damage, depending on the nature of the dispute) — if the dispute falls into an area where the policy is still a draft framework (Warranty/Refund), that's a signal those policies need to actually be finalised, not an excuse to improvise an inconsistent resolution.
4. Resolve and close the loop explicitly with the customer — don't let a dispute trail off unresolved.

## 7. Safety Incident (Injury During Handling/Sharpening)

1. **Immediate response**: stop work, assess the injury. For anything beyond a minor cut, seek appropriate medical attention — call 000 for a genuine emergency. This document does not provide medical guidance beyond that basic escalation step, which is intentional — first aid specifics should come from proper first aid training/certification, not be improvised from a business operations document.
2. **If a customer is injured** (e.g. mishandling a freshly sharpened item at hand-back): assist appropriately, document what happened, and follow up afterward.
3. **Record-keeping**: note what happened, when, and any contributing factors (e.g. a specific handling step that should be adjusted going forward) — this feeds back into `03-checklists.md`'s Safety Procedures checklist if a genuine process gap is identified.
4. **Review after any incident**: even a minor one is worth a brief review of whether the safety checklist needs updating, rather than treating it as a one-off.
