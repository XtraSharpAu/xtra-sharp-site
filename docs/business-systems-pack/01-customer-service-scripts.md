# Customer Service Scripts

Structure prompts, not word-for-word scripts — adapt naturally in conversation. For the original 10 general-flow scripts (incoming call, booking, mail-in explainer, quoting, upsell, commercial first contact, turnaround question, price objection, hand-back, declining a job), see `docs/marketing-pack/customer-scripts.md`. These 12 cover the specific scenarios requested for this pack.

## 1. Phone Calls (General Framework)

> "Xtra Sharp, this is Phil, how can I help?" — listen fully before responding. Confirm item type, quantity, and rough condition. Quote from the master pricing table (`docs/schema.md`) or offer a photo-quote if condition-dependent. Confirm drop-off or mail-in preference. Close with a clear next step ("I'll text you the address" / "send that photo through when you can").

## 2. SMS Replies

> Keep SMS replies short and action-oriented — confirm what was asked, give the next step, avoid long explanations better suited to a call.
>
> Example — price enquiry: "Hi [Name], [item] sharpening is $[price]. Drop-off or mail-in both work — let me know how you'd like to proceed."
>
> Example — availability enquiry: "Yep, I can fit that in. What day suits you?"
>
> Example — can't answer briefly: "Good question — easier to explain on a call, are you free for a quick chat? 0412 974 277"

## 3. Drop-Off Customers

> On arrival: "Thanks for coming in — let's have a quick look at what you've brought." Briefly inspect together so any pre-existing condition is noted with the customer present (this protects both sides — see `04-business-policies.md`'s Damage Policy). Confirm expected pickup time. "I'll give you a call/text when it's ready — usually same day or next day."

## 4. Mail-In Customers

> Before posting: confirm pricing, satchel size, and turnaround expectations (see `docs/long-form-content/06-mail-in-sharpening-tips.md` for the full tips list to draw on). On arrival: "Just letting you know your parcel's arrived safely, I'll check it over and get started." On completion: "All done, posting it back today/tomorrow with tracking — I'll send you the number."

## 5. Pricing Explanations

> Lead with the number, then the "why" if asked: "That's $[price] — [brief reason if relevant, e.g. 'convex takes more time to get right than a standard bevel']." If condition affects price: "I'll need to see it (or a photo) to give you an exact number, since [condition factor] makes a real difference here." Never quote vaguely if an exact price is knowable from the published list — check `docs/schema.md`'s master pricing table before improvising a number.

## 6. Handling Complaints

> Listen fully without interrupting. Acknowledge before explaining: "I hear you — that's not the result you should've gotten, let's sort it out." Ask what specifically isn't right (edge, tension, turnaround, communication) rather than assuming. Offer a concrete next step (re-sharpen at no charge if the sharpening itself was the issue, per `04-business-policies.md`'s Warranty framework — confirm actual terms there first). Never get defensive or argue the point in the moment; if something needs investigating, say so honestly: "Let me have a proper look and get back to you."

## 7. Handling Delays

> Be proactive, don't wait to be chased: "Hi [Name], quick update — [item] is going to take a bit longer than expected because [honest reason: unusually busy, a supplier delay, postal delay]. New expected time is [X]." Never let a delay go unmentioned until the customer asks. If a mail-in parcel is delayed in transit specifically, see `08-emergency-procedures.md`'s Lost Mail-In Parcel procedure if it crosses from "delayed" into "may be lost."

## 8. Handling Damaged Items (Arrived Damaged, or Damaged During Service)

> **If an item arrives already damaged** (transit damage, or damage that predates the booking): "Just letting you know [item] arrived with [specific damage] — wanted to flag it before I start any work. Did you know about this already, or is this new?" Document with a photo before proceeding. Get customer confirmation on how to proceed (sharpen as-is, attempt repair if possible, or decline per `/terms`' "sharpening may be refused" clause for unsafe/structurally compromised items).
>
> **If something goes wrong during our own process** (rare, but must be handled honestly): "I need to let you know something happened while I was working on [item] — [honest, specific explanation]." Never minimise or hide this. See `04-business-policies.md`'s Damage Policy for how liability is framed, and confirm actual resolution (repair, replacement contribution, or refund) is a genuine business decision, not scripted in advance.

## 9. Handling Incorrect Items (Wrong Item Sent, or Item Doesn't Match What Was Booked)

> "Hi [Name], just checking — I've got [what arrived] here, but the booking/quote was for [what was expected]. Can you confirm what you'd like me to do?" Don't assume and proceed on a mismatch — always confirm. If it's a simple mix-up (customer sent the wrong pair of scissors by mistake), handle it plainly and without making the customer feel bad about it: "No worries, happens all the time — let me know if you'd like me to go ahead with this one instead, or send it back."

## 10. Handling Repeat Customers

> Acknowledge the history where practical: "Good to hear from you again — last time was [item], is this the same or something different?" (Requires a basic customer record — see `docs/future-development-pack.md`'s customer-record idea.) Repeat customers are also the best candidates for the referral/loyalty asks in `07-business-growth-systems.md` — a natural, low-pressure moment to mention it, not a hard sell.

## 11. Handling Nervous Customers

> Common triggers: sending an expensive or sentimental item by mail, unfamiliarity with the process, or price sensitivity. Slow down and be explicit about what happens at each step: "Totally understand wanting to be careful with this one — here's exactly what happens: [confirm condition together / packing guidance / tracked return]." For mail-in specifically, walking through `docs/customer-education-pack/prepare-items.md`'s guidance for their specific item type directly addresses the real risk (damage in transit) rather than just offering reassurance. Never dismiss the concern as unnecessary — a nervous customer with a genuinely sentimental or expensive item has a reasonable concern.

## 12. Handling Urgent Jobs

> `/terms` already states: "Urgent jobs are available on request and quoted based on workload." Confirm honestly whether it's actually achievable before promising anything: "Let me check what I've got on — [confirm] I can turn that around by [realistic time], it may cost a bit more given the priority." Never promise an urgent turnaround you're not confident you can hit — a missed urgent promise damages trust far more than an honest "I can't get it done that fast, but here's the earliest I can."
