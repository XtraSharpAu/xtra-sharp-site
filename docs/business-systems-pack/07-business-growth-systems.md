# Business Growth Systems

Turns the ideas already drafted elsewhere in this pack into concrete, repeatable systems — cross-referencing rather than re-deriving, and adding the two genuinely new pieces (a GBP posting cadence, and upsell/cross-sell strategy) that didn't exist as systems yet.

## Customer Retention Plan

1. Basic customer record kept per job (item type, date, price) — even a simple spreadsheet, per `docs/future-development-pack.md`'s customer-record idea. This is the foundation everything else in this plan depends on.
2. Resharpening-interval reminders, timed per item type using the signals in `docs/customer-education-pack/when-to-sharpen.md` (e.g. a knife customer gets a check-in reminder after a few months, not a fixed arbitrary interval for every item type).
3. Re-engagement outreach for customers inactive 12+ months — template already drafted in `docs/marketing-pack/email-templates.md` #10.
4. Every repeat-customer interaction follows `01-customer-service-scripts.md` Section 10 (acknowledge history, natural moment to mention referral/loyalty).

## Referral System

Built on the offer structure in `docs/marketing-pack/promotional-offer-concepts.md` #2 (real reward amount left as an owner decision). Mechanics:
1. Ask at the point of highest satisfaction — hand-back of a completed job (see `docs/seo-pack.md` Section 16's review-acquisition timing logic, which applies equally well to referral asks).
2. Track who referred whom (even informally, noted against the customer record) so both parties' rewards can actually be honoured.
3. Keep the ask low-pressure and occasional — mentioned, not pushed, per `01-customer-service-scripts.md` Section 10's guidance.

## Loyalty System

Built on `docs/marketing-pack/promotional-offer-concepts.md` #8 (punch-card style — real terms left as an owner decision). Mechanics:
1. Simplest viable version for a single-operator business: track against the customer record rather than a physical/digital card system requiring separate infrastructure.
2. Reward tied to a low-cost, frequently-repeated item category (e.g. every Nth basic knife sharpen) rather than high-value/infrequent items, to keep the cost of the program predictable.

## Review Acquisition System

Full detail already in `docs/seo-pack.md` Section 16 — summary: ask at hand-back (highest satisfaction moment), direct one-click GBP link, never incentivised (see the explicit policy against paying for reviews there), respond to every review received. The review-request template is in `docs/marketing-pack/email-templates.md` #7 and the operational version in `05-templates.md` #10 above. Workflow trigger point defined in `02-workflow-documentation.md` Section 12.

## Google Business Profile Posting Plan

40 draft GBP posts already exist (`docs/marketing-pack/google-business-posts.md` + `google-business-posts-round2.md`) — this is the posting cadence to actually use them:

1. **Frequency**: 1–2 posts per week is a reasonable sustainable cadence for a single-operator business — enough to keep the profile active (which GBP's own algorithm favours) without becoming a time burden.
2. **Rotation**: cycle through service-specific posts, trust/credential posts, and local-area posts rather than posting the same category repeatedly in a row.
3. **Seasonal overlay**: swap in the relevant seasonal post from `docs/marketing-pack/seasonal-campaign-concepts.md` when a season/timing lines up (e.g. the Spring Garden Tool Prep concept posted as an actual GBP post in September).
4. **Review responses count too**: GBP activity isn't just posts — responding to every review (see Review Acquisition System above) is part of keeping the profile visibly active.

## Seasonal Promotion Plan

Full calendar already drafted in `docs/marketing-pack/seasonal-campaign-concepts.md` (20 themed campaigns with Southern Hemisphere timing). This plan is the execution layer: each seasonal campaign, when its timing arrives, should draw on the matching content already built across this pack (relevant blog post from `docs/long-form-content/` or `docs/marketing-pack/blog-posts/`, relevant social posts, relevant GBP posts) rather than creating new content from scratch each time — the content bank already exists, the seasonal plan is about *sequencing* what's already there.

## Upsell and Cross-Sell Strategies

Not previously covered elsewhere in this pack as a standalone strategy:

1. **Natural pairing prompts at booking**: per `01-customer-service-scripts.md`'s original script #5 (in `docs/marketing-pack/customer-scripts.md`) — "did you want me to take a look at any knives too?" when a customer is already booking scissors. Pair categories with real overlap: knives + garden tools (same water-cooled process, often same household), scissors + clipper blades (same machine-guided process, often same salon/groomer).
2. **Batch/commercial upsell**: a one-off individual customer who mentions running a business (salon, restaurant, school) is a natural prompt to mention batch pricing and scheduled cycles (see `02-workflow-documentation.md`'s Drop-Off and Mail-In workflows).
3. **Never upsell beyond genuine need**: an item that doesn't actually need sharpening yet shouldn't be sold a sharpen just because the customer is already present — this would undermine the trust-based positioning this whole pack is built around (see `docs/branding-pack.md`'s "factual over promotional" voice trait). Upsell means surfacing a real, relevant additional service the customer may not have thought of, not manufacturing demand.
