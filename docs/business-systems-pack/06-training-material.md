# Training Material

Written for onboarding anyone new to inspection/customer-service duties at Xtra Sharp — currently a single-operator business, so this is as much a reference/consistency document for the owner as it is training material for a future hire.

## 1. Inspecting Knives

**What to check**: edge condition (even wear vs. chips vs. rolled edge), tip damage, handle/tang looseness, rust/corrosion, current angle vs. expected angle for that knife type. **How**: visual inspection under good light, running a thumbnail lightly along the spine (never the edge) to gauge general condition, checking for the "white line" test (light reflecting off a dull edge). **Reference**: `03-checklists.md` Section 1, `docs/technical-pack.md`'s angle reference table.

## 2. Inspecting Scissors

**What to check**: bevel vs. convex edge type, tension (open and close the scissors — should move smoothly without wobble or excess resistance), ride line alignment, pivot condition, tip/handle damage. **How**: operate the scissors through a full open-close motion before any sharpening to establish a baseline. **Reference**: `03-checklists.md` Section 2, `docs/long-form-content/02-scissors-sharpening.md`.

## 3. Inspecting Clipper Blades

**What to check**: blade type (A5/wide/large-animal), top-to-bottom blade alignment, edge condition on both blades, nicks/chips, mounting/hub fit. **How**: visually compare the teeth of both blades for alignment before removal from the clipper body if possible (easier to assess in situ). **Reference**: `03-checklists.md` Section 3, `docs/long-form-content/03-clipper-blade-maintenance.md`.

## 4. Inspecting Garden Tools

**What to check**: pivot bolt condition (secateurs/shears), handle condition (axes/tomahawks — cracks and looseness are safety issues, flag before any sharpening), edge condition, rust/sap residue. **How**: check the handle and pivot before assessing the edge — a compromised handle changes whether the tool should even be sharpened and returned for use. **Reference**: `03-checklists.md` Section 4, `docs/troubleshooting-pack/garden-tools.md`.

## 5. Inspecting Processor Blades

**What to check**: blade type/size, edge condition, mounting hub/shaft fit (visual only — not adjusted), unusual wear or damage. **How**: always power down and disconnect equipment before any handling; inspect the blade itself, not the machine (installation/electrical issues are outside scope — see `docs/long-form-content/05-processor-blade-safety.md`). **Reference**: `03-checklists.md` Section 5.

## 6. Identifying Damage (General, Across Item Types)

**Categories to distinguish**:
- **Normal wear** (even dulling from use) — not damage, standard sharpening addresses it.
- **Minor damage** (small chips, light rust, minor rolled edge) — typically addressable during standard sharpening, per `/terms`' "minor chips, bends, and tip repairs are included where possible."
- **Severe damage** (large chips, significant bends, structural cracks in handles) — quoted individually per `/terms`, may not be safely sharpenable.
- **Pre-existing vs. new**: always establish whether damage predates the booking (found at intake) or is a genuine concern about work performed by Xtra Sharp — these are handled completely differently (see Training Guide 7 and `01-customer-service-scripts.md` Section 8).

## 7. Explaining Damage to Customers

**Principles**: always disclose before proceeding, never after. Be specific, not vague ("there's a chip about 2cm from the tip" not "it's a bit damaged"). Photograph before discussing where practical — this protects both the customer and the business by creating a clear record. Never assign blame speculatively ("this looks like it was dropped") — describe what's observed, not a guess at cause, unless the customer volunteers that information themselves. **Script reference**: `01-customer-service-scripts.md` Section 8.

## 8. Sharpening Workflow Training

Walk through `docs/technical-pack.md`'s full sharpening workflow (intake → assessment → method selection → restoration → edge-forming → burr removal → tension/alignment where applicable → cleaning → QC → return) alongside the item-specific workflows in `02-workflow-documentation.md`. Emphasise burr removal and heat management specifically — these are the two steps most commonly rushed in lower-quality sharpening (see `docs/long-form-content/01-knife-sharpening.md` and `03-water-cooled-vs-dry-grinding.md`), and getting them right is the core quality differentiator this business is built on.

## 9. Customer Service Training

Walk through `01-customer-service-scripts.md`'s 12 scenarios. Core principles to internalise rather than memorise word-for-word: transparency before speed (always disclose issues before proceeding), proactive communication over waiting to be asked, and honesty about capacity (never promise a turnaround — especially urgent jobs — without genuine confidence it can be met). Role-play the complaint-handling and damaged-item scenarios specifically before handling a real one — these are the highest-stakes interactions and benefit most from practice.
