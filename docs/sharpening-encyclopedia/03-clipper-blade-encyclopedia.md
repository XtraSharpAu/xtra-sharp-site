# Clipper Blade Encyclopedia

## Anatomy of Clipper Blades *(new — structured reference)*

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Cutting blade** | The moving blade (usually the top blade), driven back and forth by the clipper motor. |
| **Comb blade** | The stationary blade (usually the bottom blade) with teeth that guide hair into the cutting blade's path. |
| **Teeth** | The individual points along each blade's edge — hair is sheared in the gap between the cutting blade's and comb blade's teeth as they pass each other. |
| **Alignment** | How precisely the cutting blade's teeth track against the comb blade's teeth through the full range of motion — see "Blade Alignment Theory" below. |
| **Tracking** | The path the cutting blade follows relative to the comb blade during operation; related to but distinct from static alignment. |
| **Mounting hub/tab** | The point where the blade set attaches to the clipper body — fit here affects performance independent of edge sharpness. |
| **Size number** | Manufacturer-assigned number indicating cut length (varies by brand) — see `docs/long-form-content/13-clipper-blade-sizes-guide.md` for how this relates to blade width categories (A5, wide, large-animal). |

## Blade Alignment Theory

A clipper blade set only cuts correctly when the cutting blade's teeth and the comb blade's teeth meet with precise, consistent contact along their full width, throughout the cutting blade's full range of motion — not just at a single point. Misalignment, even slight, causes hair to be pulled or caught rather than sheared cleanly, and increases friction (and therefore heat and wear) during operation. This is why clipper blade sharpening addresses alignment and tracking as part of the same process as edge sharpness, not as a separate issue — see `docs/technical-pack.md`'s "Machine-Guided Sharpening Science" section and `docs/long-form-content/03-clipper-blade-maintenance.md`'s "Blade Pair, Not Just 'The Blade'" section for the full explanation.

## Clipper Blade Sharpening Myths vs. Facts

- **Myth: "A sharp-feeling blade is a well-aligned blade."** Fact: a blade can be individually sharp and still perform poorly if misaligned with its pair — see the Anatomy table above.
- **Myth: "Larger/tougher blades (large-animal) don't need sharpening as often."** Fact: tougher material can mean more frequent nicks from debris, not necessarily less frequent sharpening — see `docs/long-form-content/13-clipper-blade-sizes-guide.md`.
- **Myth: "Any blade works for any job, as long as it's sharp."** Fact: using the wrong blade category for a job (e.g. a narrow A5 forced through large-animal-grade coat) accelerates wear regardless of how recently it was sharpened.
