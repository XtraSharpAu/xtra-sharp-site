## Anatomy of Processor Blades

Processor blades vary significantly by equipment make/model (home units vs. large industrial equipment) — general anatomy: cutting edge, mounting hub/shaft (where the blade attaches to the drive mechanism), and blade body. Unlike knives or scissors, there's no single standardised "anatomy" across processor blades the way there is for more consistently-shaped tools — correct fit and installation is always specific to the individual equipment's manufacturer documentation, not a generalisable reference (see the safety scope note above).

## Processor Blade Sharpening Myths vs. Facts

- **Myth: "A blunt processor blade is just a quality issue, not a safety one."** Fact: a blunt blade forces the motor to work harder and can produce unpredictable results — see `docs/long-form-content/05-processor-blade-safety.md`.
- **Myth: "Industrial blades are built tough enough that sharpening schedules don't matter much."** Fact: high-volume commercial use still causes real wear requiring a genuine maintenance schedule — see `docs/long-form-content/15-commercial-kitchen-blade-maintenance-scheduling.md`.
- **Myth: "Any sharpening service can safely handle processor blade reinstallation."** Fact: reinstallation is equipment-specific and outside a sharpening service's scope unless explicitly agreed — see `docs/business-systems-pack/02-workflow-documentation.md`'s Processor Blade workflow.
