# Scissors Sharpening Encyclopedia

## Scissor Anatomy *(new — structured reference)*

A pair of scissors has more named parts than most people think about day to day — knowing them makes the rest of this encyclopedia's cross-references easier to follow:

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Blade** | The full cutting portion of each half, from pivot to tip. |
| **Edge** | The sharpened cutting line along the inside of each blade. |
| **Ride line** (also "shear line") | The point of contact between the two blades as they close — where the actual cutting/shearing happens. A correctly set ride line means the blades touch and glide cleanly along their whole length as they close, not just at one point. |
| **Pivot** | The screw or rivet joining the two blade halves, around which they rotate. Controls tension. |
| **Set** | The slight curve or bend built into each blade half (usually more pronounced on one blade) that allows the ride line to stay in contact as the blades close, despite the blades themselves being basically straight. |
| **Tension** | How firmly the pivot holds the two halves together — affects how the blades meet through the cutting motion (see "Tension Theory" below). |
| **Finger/thumb holes (bows)** | The handle loops — not functionally part of the cutting mechanism, but their fit affects how much control and pressure a user can apply through the blades. |
| **Tang/shank** | The section connecting the blade to the handle. |
| **Tip** | The point where the two blades end — often the first place wear or misalignment becomes noticeable, since it's furthest from the pivot. |

## Scissor Sharpening Myths vs. Facts

The "bevel and convex are basically the same job" myth is covered in `docs/long-form-content/08-sharpening-myths.md`. Additional scissor-specific myths not previously documented:

- **Myth: "A scissors sharpen only involves the edge."** Fact: tension and alignment matter as much as the edge — see the anatomy table above and `docs/long-form-content/02-scissors-sharpening.md`.
- **Myth: "Thinning scissors don't need the same precision as main shears."** Fact: thinning scissors need the same care — see `docs/troubleshooting-pack/scissors.md`.
- **Myth: "If scissors still close, they don't need sharpening."** Fact: closing smoothly is a tension/pivot question, separate from edge sharpness — scissors can close perfectly and still cut poorly if the edge itself is dull.
