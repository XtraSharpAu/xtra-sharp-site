# Sharpening Tools & Equipment

## Important Scope Note

This is **general industry/trade education** about categories of equipment used across the professional sharpening trade broadly — it is **not a claim about which specific machines, brands, or models Xtra Sharp owns and operates**. No verified information about Xtra Sharp's actual specific equipment exists in this project's source material, and this document doesn't invent any. If this content is ever used in customer-facing material, any Xtra-Sharp-specific equipment claims should come from the business owner directly, not from this generic category description.

## Water-Cooled Machines

Power grinding equipment that incorporates a continuous water feed across the blade and abrasive contact point during operation, carrying away frictional heat as it's generated (see `06-science-and-theory.md`'s water-cooled vs. dry section for the underlying physics). Used across the trade for knives, garden tools, and other items where temper protection during grinding matters most.

## Belt Sharpeners

Equipment using a continuous abrasive belt (rather than a fixed wheel) running over the blade at a controlled angle. Belts can be swapped for different grits, allowing a progression from coarse (damage correction/restoration) to fine (edge refinement) on the same machine. Common across the professional sharpening trade for knives and similar single-bevel blades.

## Grinding Wheels

Fixed, rotating abrasive wheels — one of the oldest forms of powered sharpening equipment, tracing back to the Industrial Revolution's introduction of powered grinding (see `01-knife-encyclopedia.md`'s History section). Wheel composition (abrasive material and grit, see `06-science-and-theory.md`) varies by intended use, from coarse restoration wheels to fine finishing wheels.

## Honing Systems

Equipment or fixtures (ranging from simple guided honing rods to more elaborate fixture-based systems) used for the lighter maintenance pass — realigning an existing edge rather than removing significant material — distinct from full sharpening equipment. Relevant to both professional touch-up work and to customer-facing honing advice (see `docs/customer-education-pack/maintain-sharp-edges.md`).

## Polishing Systems

Fine-finishing equipment (often a soft or compound-charged wheel/belt) used after the main edge-forming and grit progression is complete, to refine the final edge surface finish. More relevant to some blade types and finishes than others — not every sharpening job requires a separate polishing stage.

## Inspection Tools

Ranges from simple (good lighting, a loupe or magnifier for close edge inspection, a straightedge for checking evenness) to more elaborate (angle-measuring gauges, microscopes for detailed burr/edge inspection in more specialised settings). The QC checklist in `docs/technical-pack.md` describes what's being checked; this category is the general tooling used to check it.

## Safety Equipment

General categories relevant across the sharpening trade: eye protection (standard for any grinding operation, given sparks and debris), appropriate gloves for handling (noting the specific guidance in `docs/long-form-content/05-processor-blade-safety.md` that gloves don't fully protect against a genuinely sharp edge — handling technique matters more than glove material for edge safety specifically), and hearing protection where equipment noise levels warrant it. See `docs/business-systems-pack/03-checklists.md`'s Safety Procedures checklist for the operational safety practices this equipment supports.
