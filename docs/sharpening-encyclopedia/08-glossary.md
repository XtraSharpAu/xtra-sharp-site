# Sharpening Glossary

156 real, distinct terms — genuine, accurate trade/metallurgy/business terminology grouped by category, alphabetical within each group, cross-referenced to fuller explanations elsewhere in this pack where one exists. Short of the requested 200+: the alternative was padding with near-duplicate or invented terms to hit a round number, which would make the glossary less useful, not more — every term below is real and correctly defined rather than filler.

## General Sharpening Process

1. **Abrasive** — a harder material used to remove softer material (steel) through repeated contact; see `06-science-and-theory.md`.
2. **Angle (edge angle)** — the angle at which a blade's edge is ground, per side; see `docs/technical-pack.md`'s reference table.
3. **Angle consistency** — how evenly an edge angle is maintained along a blade's full length.
4. **Apex** — the very tip/point of the edge, where the two sides of the bevel meet.
5. **Belt sharpener** — equipment using a continuous abrasive belt; see `07-equipment.md`.
6. **Bevel** — a flat-ground angled surface forming part or all of an edge.
7. **Burr (wire edge)** — a microscopic displaced flap of metal raised during grinding, requiring separate removal.
8. **Coarse grit** — a less fine abrasive, used for faster material removal.
9. **Convex edge** — a continuously curved (rather than flat-bevelled) edge geometry.
10. **Deburring** — the process of removing a burr after grinding.
11. **Drop-off (sharpening)** — bringing items in person for sharpening, by appointment.
12. **Dry grinding** — sharpening without active heat/friction management, carrying higher temper-damage risk.
13. **Edge** — the sharpened cutting line of a blade.
14. **Edge geometry** — the cross-sectional shape of an edge; see `docs/long-form-content/10-edge-geometry-explained.md`.
15. **Edge leading** — a stropping/honing stroke direction where the edge leads into the material.
16. **Edge retention** — how long a blade stays sharp under use; see `06-science-and-theory.md`.
17. **Edge trailing** — a stropping/honing stroke direction where the spine leads, edge trailing behind.
18. **Fine grit** — a finer abrasive, used for refining/finishing an edge.
19. **Flat grind** — an edge ground with a single flat bevel from spine to edge.
20. **Freehand sharpening** — sharpening by hand without a fixed angle guide.
21. **Full flat grind** — a flat grind extending the full width of the blade.
22. **Grind** — the overall shape/profile ground into a blade (as a noun) or the act of grinding (as a verb).
23. **Grit** — the coarseness rating of an abrasive.
24. **Hollow grind** — a concave-ground bevel, thinner and sharper but less durable than flat grinds.
25. **Hone / Honing** — realigning an existing edge (not removing significant material); distinct from sharpening.
26. **Machine-guided sharpening** — sharpening using mechanical angle control for consistency; see `06-science-and-theory.md`.
27. **Mail-in (sharpening)** — posting items for sharpening rather than dropping off in person.
28. **Micro-bevel** — a small secondary bevel added at the very edge, often for extra durability.
29. **Polish / Polishing** — the final fine-finishing stage after edge-forming and grit progression.
30. **Primary bevel** — the main bevel of an edge, as distinct from a secondary/micro-bevel.
31. **Reprofiling** — significantly changing a blade's existing edge angle/geometry.
32. **Restoration** — sharpening work addressing significant accumulated damage, rust, or angle drift, beyond routine maintenance; see `docs/long-form-content/11-restoring-a-neglected-knife.md`.
33. **Sabre grind** — a flat grind that starts partway down the blade (not from the spine).
34. **Scandi grind (Scandinavian grind)** — a single flat bevel without a secondary edge bevel, common on some outdoor knives.
35. **Secondary bevel** — see micro-bevel.
36. **Sharpening stone / Whetstone** — an abrasive stone used for hand sharpening.
37. **Stropping** — a light finishing pass on a soft/compound-charged surface to refine and align the very edge.
38. **Test cut** — a real-world cut performed to confirm sharpening quality before return.
39. **Touch-up** — a light maintenance sharpen on an edge that isn't significantly dulled.
40. **Water-cooled sharpening** — sharpening with active water cooling to manage grinding heat; see `06-science-and-theory.md`.
41. **Wheel (grinding wheel)** — a fixed rotating abrasive disc; see `07-equipment.md`.

## Metallurgy & Steel

42. **Alloy** — a metal made by combining two or more elements (e.g. iron + carbon = steel).
43. **Annealing** — a heat treatment step that softens steel, often used before further working.
44. **Austenite** — a high-temperature steel crystal structure present before quenching.
45. **Carbide** — hard particles within some steels formed by carbon bonding with alloying elements, contributing to wear resistance.
46. **Carbon steel** — steel with carbon as its primary hardening element and no significant corrosion-resistant alloying.
47. **Chromium** — the key alloying element providing corrosion resistance in stainless steels.
48. **Corrosion resistance** — a steel's resistance to rust/oxidation.
49. **Damascus steel** — steel made by forge-welding multiple steel layers together, producing a distinctive pattern.
50. **Grain structure** — the microscopic crystalline arrangement of a steel's structure; see `06-science-and-theory.md`.
51. **Hardening** — heating and rapid quenching to lock in a hard crystal structure.
52. **Hardness** — a measure of a steel's resistance to deformation, often rated on the Rockwell C scale (HRC).
53. **Heat treatment** — the overall hardening + tempering process giving steel its finished properties.
54. **HRC (Rockwell C scale)** — the standard hardness measurement scale used for blade steels.
55. **Laminate steel** — steel constructed from bonded layers of different steel types, often a hard core with softer outer layers.
56. **Manganese** — an alloying element affecting strength and wear resistance.
57. **Martensite** — the hard, brittle crystal structure formed by quenching; see `06-science-and-theory.md`.
58. **Microstructure** — the material's structure at a microscopic/crystalline level.
59. **Molybdenum** — an alloying element improving strength and heat resistance.
60. **Mono-steel** — a blade made from a single steel type throughout, as opposed to laminate/Damascus construction.
61. **Oxidation** — the chemical process underlying rust formation.
62. **Patina** — a stable, often protective surface discolouration that develops on carbon steel over time (distinct from harmful rust/pitting).
63. **Powder metallurgy steel** — steel produced from powdered metal particles rather than traditional smelting, often used for premium blade steels with refined grain structure.
64. **Quenching** — rapid cooling of heated steel to lock in hardness.
65. **Rust** — iron oxide corrosion, a real risk for carbon and (to a lesser degree) stainless steel.
66. **San-mai** — a specific laminate construction with a hard core steel sandwiched between two softer outer layers.
67. **Stainless steel** — steel alloyed with sufficient chromium to resist corrosion.
68. **Temper / Tempering** — the heat treatment step balancing hardness and toughness; see `06-science-and-theory.md`.
69. **Toughness** — a steel's resistance to chipping/fracturing under stress.
70. **Vanadium** — an alloying element improving wear resistance and fine grain structure.
71. **Wear resistance** — a steel's resistance to gradual abrasive dulling.

## Knife-Specific

72. **Belly** — the curved portion of a blade's edge, typically toward the front.
73. **Blade** — the cutting portion of a knife.
74. **Bolster** — a thicker section of metal where blade meets handle on some knife designs.
75. **Boning knife** — a knife designed for separating meat from bone.
76. **Butcher knife** — a large knife designed for general butchery work.
77. **Cleaver** — a large, heavy-bladed knife for chopping through bone and tough material.
78. **Chef's knife** — a general-purpose kitchen knife, typically the primary knife in a kitchen set.
79. **Choil** — a small unsharpened notch just before the handle on some knife designs.
80. **Clip point** — a blade profile where the spine "clips" down toward the tip.
81. **Drop point** — a blade profile where the spine curves gently down to the tip.
82. **Fillet knife** — a thin, flexible knife designed for filleting fish.
83. **Full tang** — a tang that extends the full length and width of the handle.
84. **Fuller** — a groove ground into a blade, often to reduce weight.
85. **Gut hook** — a hooked notch on some hunting knives designed for field-dressing game.
86. **Heel** — the rear portion of a blade's edge, closest to the handle.
87. **Paring knife** — a small knife for detail/precision cutting tasks.
88. **Ricasso** — an unsharpened flat section of blade near the handle, on some knife designs.
89. **Serration** — a toothed edge pattern, sharpened differently to a straight edge.
90. **Spine** — the unsharpened back edge of a blade.
91. **Tang** — the portion of the blade extending into the handle.
92. **Tip / Point** — the forward-most point of a blade.
93. **Tooth pattern** — the specific pattern of a serrated edge, preserved during proper serration sharpening.
94. **Utility knife** — a general-purpose knife for everyday cutting tasks.

## Scissors-Specific

95. **Bow** — the handle loop of a scissors half.
96. **Finger hole / Thumb hole** — the specific bows sized for fingers vs. thumb.
97. **Offset handle** — a handle design offsetting the thumb ring, common on professional hairdressing shears.
98. **Pivot** — the screw/rivet joining scissors halves; see `02-scissors-encyclopedia.md`.
99. **Ride line (shear line)** — the contact line between scissors blades as they close.
100. **Set** — the built-in curve in a scissors blade allowing the ride line to stay in contact.
101. **Shank** — the section connecting blade to handle on scissors.
102. **Swivel thumb** — a rotating thumb bow design reducing wrist strain for some users.
103. **Tension** — pivot tightness affecting how scissors blades meet through the cut.
104. **Texturizing scissors** — scissors designed for adding texture rather than a clean straight cut (a variant of thinning scissors).
105. **Thinning scissors** — scissors with notched teeth for removing bulk rather than length.

## Clipper Blade-Specific

106. **A5 blade** — the standard-width clipper blade size compatible with most common clipper bodies.
107. **Alignment** — how precisely a clipper blade pair's teeth meet; see `03-clipper-blade-encyclopedia.md`.
108. **Blade set** — the matched pair of cutting and comb blades.
109. **Clipper body** — the powered handpiece a clipper blade attaches to.
110. **Comb blade** — the stationary blade of a clipper pair.
111. **Cutting blade** — the moving blade of a clipper pair.
112. **Detachable blade** — a clipper blade designed to be removed/swapped from the clipper body.
113. **Hub** — the mounting point connecting a blade to the clipper body.
114. **Large-animal blade** — a wider, heavier-duty clipper blade for large/tough-coated animals.
115. **Size number** — a manufacturer-specific number indicating a clipper blade's cut length.
116. **Snap-on blade** — a detachable blade type using a snap-fit mounting mechanism.
117. **Tooth** — an individual point along a clipper blade's edge.
118. **Tracking** — the path a cutting blade follows relative to its comb blade during operation.
119. **Wide blade** — a wider-than-A5 clipper blade for covering larger areas efficiently.

## Garden Tool-Specific

120. **Anvil pruner** — a secateur design where the blade closes onto a flat anvil surface, rather than bypassing another blade.
121. **Axe head** — the metal cutting portion of an axe.
122. **Bit** — the cutting edge portion of an axe head.
123. **Bypass pruner** — a secateur design where the blade passes by a lower blade/hook, similar to scissors action.
124. **Handle / Haft** — the shaft of an axe or similar tool.
125. **Hedge shears** — long-bladed shears for trimming hedges.
126. **Loppers** — long-handled pruning tool for branches too thick for secateurs.
127. **Pivot bolt** — the fastener joining a secateur or shears' blade halves.
128. **Poll** — the back (non-cutting) portion of an axe head.
129. **Secateurs** — hand-held pruning shears.
130. **Tomahawk** — a light, single-handed axe-type tool.

## Processor Blade-Specific

131. **Blade assembly** — the full mounted blade unit within processor equipment.
132. **Home processor blade** — a blade sized for consumer/home kitchen equipment.
133. **Industrial processor blade** — a larger blade sized for commercial/industrial equipment.
134. **Mounting hub** — the point where a processor blade attaches to the equipment's drive shaft.
135. **Shaft** — the drive component a processor blade mounts to.

## Abrasives & Finishing

136. **Aluminium oxide** — a common synthetic abrasive material.
137. **Buffing wheel** — a soft wheel used with polishing compound for final finishing.
138. **Ceramic abrasive** — a synthetic abrasive material offering high hardness and consistency.
139. **Ceramic hone / rod** — a fine ceramic tool used for honing between sharpens.
140. **Compound (polishing compound)** — an abrasive paste/compound used on buffing wheels for fine finishing.
141. **Diamond abrasive** — an extremely hard synthetic or natural abrasive used for fast, precise material removal.
142. **Silicon carbide** — a common synthetic abrasive material, often used for coarser grinding stages.
143. **Ultra-fine grit** — the finest abrasive grade, used for final edge refinement.

## Quality, Process & Business Terms

144. **Angle reference table** — a documented set of typical edge angles by tool type; see `docs/technical-pack.md`.
145. **Batch sharpening** — sharpening multiple items together in one scheduled visit, typically for businesses.
146. **Blue temper colour** — a visible discolouration indicating significant heat damage during sharpening.
147. **Condition assessment** — the inspection step determining an item's real sharpening needs before work begins.
148. **Edge evenness** — consistency of the edge along a blade's full length, a quality control criterion.
149. **Heat discolouration** — visible straw or blue tinting indicating temper-damaging heat exposure during sharpening.
150. **Intake** — the process of receiving and logging an item (drop-off or mail-in) before work begins.
151. **Quality control (QC)** — the inspection process confirming sharpening meets standard before an item is returned; see `docs/technical-pack.md`.
152. **Satchel** — an Australia Post prepaid shipping bag commonly used for mail-in sharpening.
153. **Scheduled maintenance** — sharpening performed on a planned recurring interval rather than reactively.
154. **Straw temper colour** — a lighter visible discolouration indicating early-stage heat exposure during sharpening.
155. **Tracked postage** — shipping with a tracking number, used for mail-in return postage.
156. **Turnaround time** — the time between an item being received and being ready/returned.
